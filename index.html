    Rules of Remorse Chess

    §1 Summary

    Remorse Chess is orthodox chess with one addition. The first time a piece captures, it rolls a six-sided die, and the result decides how that piece lives with what it has done.

    Objective. Checkmate the opposing king. Every standard rule applies unless a piece's state says otherwise.

    Original Concept. The psychological capture consequences and remorse rules were created by @bardelo_bardalini.

    §2 The reaction roll

    Each piece rolls exactly once per game, on its first capture. The state it receives is permanent.

    D6StateEffect
    1DevastatedCannot move. Removes itself unless absolved within 3 of its owner's turns.
    2–3PacifistMoves normally. Cannot capture until absolved.
    4–5UnaffectedStandard rules.
    6PsychopathOne extra move after each capture.

    §3 States

    3.1 Devastated

    The piece cannot move or capture. A bishop must move adjacent and spend one full turn absolving it within 3 of its owner's turns. If no one comes, the piece captures itself.

    3.2 Pacifist

    A guilt token is placed under the piece. It moves normally but cannot capture enemy pieces. A bishop must move adjacent and spend one full turn to lift the token.

    Bishops. A bishop that turns Pacifist spends its next turn in meditation and then absolves itself.

    3.3 Psychopath and Bloodlust

    The piece takes one additional move immediately after each capture. At three total kills it falls into Bloodlust: it may only move by capturing, it may capture friendly pieces, and it may never capture its own king.

    §4 Absolution

    Only bishops can absolve. Absolution is not possible while your king is in check. If a side loses every bishop, it cannot absolve until a pawn promotes to a bishop.
